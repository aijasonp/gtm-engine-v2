# Knowledge Structuring Skill

## Overview

Unstructured notes are dead knowledge. This skill takes raw inputs — call notes, research dumps, meeting transcripts, Slack threads, email threads, scattered bullet points — and transforms them into organized, reusable assets with clear hierarchy, actionable sections, and logical flow.

Applied to BDR work: call notes become deal briefs, competitor intel becomes battlecards, persona research becomes ICP profiles, win/loss patterns become playbooks.

---

## Instructions

### Inputs accepted
- Raw call notes or transcript text
- Messy bullet points or stream-of-consciousness notes
- Multiple documents or sources to synthesize
- A topic area with rough content to organize (e.g., "here's everything I know about our CISO persona")

### Step 1 — Identify the output type
Before structuring, determine what this needs to become:

| Input type | → Output format |
|------------|----------------|
| Call notes | Deal brief / follow-up brief |
| Competitor intel | Battlecard (use Competitive Intelligence skill for full output) |
| Persona research | ICP profile |
| Process description | SOP / playbook |
| Market research | Insight summary |
| Scattered talking points | Structured narrative or FAQ |

### Step 2 — Extract and categorize
Parse the raw content and sort every piece of information into:
- **Facts** (confirmed, sourced)
- **Inferences** (reasonable conclusions from facts — label as such)
- **Questions** (things unknown that matter)
- **Actions** (tasks that should result from this information)

### Step 3 — Build the hierarchy
Apply the appropriate structure based on output type:

**Deal Brief (from call notes)**
```
DEAL BRIEF: [Company] | [Date]
Rep: | Stage: | Next step:

What we learned
  - About the business
  - About the pain
  - About the buying process

What they care about (top 3)
  1.
  2.
  3.

Open questions
  -

Risks / red flags
  -

Recommended next action
  -
```

**ICP Profile (from persona research)**
```
ICP PROFILE: [Title] at [Company Type]

Who they are
  - Day-to-day responsibilities
  - What they're measured on
  - Team size / reporting structure

What keeps them up at night
  - Primary pain points
  - Common triggers that make them look for solutions

How they buy
  - Typical evaluation process
  - Decision criteria
  - Who else is involved

What resonates
  - Messaging that lands
  - Proof points they care about
  - Formats they respond to (case studies, benchmarks, demos)

What to avoid
  - Approaches that fall flat
  - Objections they always raise
```

**Playbook Section (from process notes)**
```
PLAYBOOK: [Process Name]

When to use this
Trigger conditions:

Step-by-step
  1.
  2.
  3.

Decision points
  - If [condition] → [action]

Common mistakes to avoid
  -

Success looks like
  -
```

### Step 4 — Surface gaps
After structuring, explicitly call out:
- What information is missing that would make this more useful
- What assumptions were made that should be verified
- What follow-up questions would sharpen the picture

---

## Constraints

- Label inferences clearly — never present an inference as a confirmed fact.
- Preserve the rep's voice in deal briefs — don't over-sanitize.
- Keep hierarchy to 3 levels max. Deeper than that is bureaucracy, not structure.
- If the raw input is too thin to structure meaningfully, say so and ask for more.
- Actions must be specific: "follow up" is not an action; "send pricing comparison by Friday" is.
